# Intrinsic finite representation of raw-variation conditionals

## Scope and prior work

This note gives necessary and sufficient conditions on **abstract conditional
systems on a finite world set**, without supplying coordinates, for an exact representation
by the local-support raw-variation semantics of the preceding note. The
conditions combine an existential Boolean completion of observable equivalence
relations with a coordinate-free verifier recursion. They yield reconstruction,
uniqueness of the conditional extension from atomic data, and an NP upper bound
for finite explicitly specified observations. They are not a finite equational
axiomatization or an infinite-model representation theorem. Priority of the
combined result is not established.

The product-decomposition ingredient is standard factor-equivalence theory.
Anagnostopoulou-Merkouri, Bailey and Cameron [ABC], Sections 2.3–2.5, recall the
older representation of uniform distributive lattices of commuting partitions
as poset block structures. Team independence [GV] already expresses the
coordinate-amalgamation property behind our earlier rectangularity theorem.
Local dependence logic [GP], ceteris-paribus modalities [GLS], and assignment
dynamic logic [BHT] already study closely related transition structures.
Jackson–Stokes [JS] give complete axioms for partial-function override/update.
Neither Cartesian decomposition nor overwrite itself is claimed new here.

The extra issue addressed here is simultaneous **truth-label locality** and
the full **positive/negative exact-verifier recursion**. A geometrically valid
family of coordinate equivalences need not admit the required local labels.

## 1. Input, with no coordinates

Let W be a nonempty finite set, N=|W|, and let p_1,…,p_m be named atoms with
truth sets P_p⊆W. Normal formulas are the free syntax generated by ¬, ∧, ∨;
classical truth is computed from the P_p, but classically equivalent formulas
are not identified as antecedents.

An abstract conditional system supplies a relation R_A⊆W×W for every normal
formula A. Its conditional is interpreted by

    w ⊨ A>C  iff  R_A(w)⊆[[C]].

Consequents may be nested. A representation means a bijection
W≅∏_{i∈I}D_i, atomic supports S_p⊆I, and atomic truth local to S_p, such that
**every supplied R_A** equals the raw exact-verifier relation in those
coordinates. This is an isomorphic representation on the same worlds, not
merely preservation at a distinguished point or simulation on extra worlds.

Equivalently, one can start with operators c_A:P(W)→P(W). They have relational
representations exactly when c_A(W)=W and each preserves binary intersections.
Indeed, on finite W,

    w R_A v  iff  w∉c_A(W\{v}).

Every X is the intersection of W\{v} over v∉X, which proves the equivalence.
This is the usual finite modal/Boolean representation step, not a novelty claim.

Write Δ for equality, U=W×W, Eq(W) for the equivalence-relation lattice,
E∧F=E∩F, and E∨F for equivalence closure of E∪F. Relational composition is
written E∘F; equivalences commute when E∘F=F∘E.

Recover the candidate support relations solely from atomic conditionals:

    E_p = R_p ∪ R_¬p,
    H_p = {(u,v): (u∈P_p iff v∈P_p)}.

Here H_p records agreement in the observable truth value, not a coordinate.

## 2. The completion and generation conditions

**(A) Atomic reset.** Every E_p is an equivalence relation, and

    R_p = E_p ∩ (W×P_p),
    R_¬p = E_p ∩ (W×(W\P_p)).

**(B) Local Boolean completion.** There exists a bounded Boolean sublattice
𝔅⊆Eq(W), with bottom Δ and top U, containing all E_p, such that:

* all its members commute;
* if E_p^* is the Boolean complement of E_p **in 𝔅**, then E_p^*⊆H_p.

No coordinates, coordinate domains, or preselected covering partitions are
part of the input. Condition (B) is a finite relation-completion problem on W;
it quantifies over equivalence relations, not over possible-world embeddings.
The completion need not be unique. For N=1 the one-element Boolean algebra
Δ=U is allowed, giving a zero-coordinate singleton product.

**(G) Generated exact verifiers.** A typed verifier is a pair (E,Q), with
E∈Eq(W) and Q⊆W. For families of pairs define

    X ★ Y = {(E∨F, Q∩T): (E,Q)∈X, (F,T)∈Y}.

Starting from T_p={(E_p,P_p)} and F_p={(E_p,W\P_p)}, define:

    T_¬A=F_A,                         F_¬A=T_A;
    T_(A∧B)=T_A ★ T_B,               F_(A∧B)=F_A∪F_B∪(F_A ★ F_B);
    T_(A∨B)=T_A∪T_B∪(T_A ★ T_B),     F_(A∨B)=F_A ★ F_B.

Require, for every A,

    R_A = ⋃{ E∩(W×Q) : (E,Q)∈T_A }.

The joins used here are joins in Eq(W), hence independent of the selected
completion. Empty Q branches are harmless. The fusion branch in disjunction
is retained; replacing it by ordinary union changes the target semantics.

## 3. Complete finite representation theorem

**Theorem 1.** An abstract conditional system on finite nonempty W is
representable by local-support raw variation on a full finite product if and
only if it satisfies (A), (B), and (G).

**Necessity.** In a coordinate representation, let E_S relate worlds agreeing
outside S. The family {E_S:S⊆I}, after discarding singleton coordinates, is a
bounded Boolean sublattice of commuting equivalences. Atomic truth and falsity
partition the endpoints available when varying S_p, so their relation union
is exactly E_{S_p}; this proves (A). Its complement permits variation outside
S_p, which preserves P_p by locality, proving (B). In the verifier recursion,
support union corresponds to equivalence join and simultaneous verification
to truth-set intersection. Structural induction proves (G). □

**Sufficiency: reconstruct the worlds.** Choose a completion 𝔅, with Boolean
atoms a_1,…,a_k, and let K_i=a_i^* be its coatoms. Define D_i=W/K_i and

    h(w)=([w]_{K_1},…,[w]_{K_k}).

This map is injective because ∩_i K_i=Δ. To show surjectivity, prescribe one
class of each K_i. Suppose a point x satisfies the first r class choices and
let J=∩_{i≤r}K_i. Boolean distributivity gives J∨K_{r+1}=U. Since J and
K_{r+1} commute, their composition equals their join, hence U. For any y in
the next prescribed class, choose z with x J z K_{r+1} y. Then z retains the
first r choices and satisfies the next one. Induction realizes every tuple.
For k=0, W is a singleton and h is the unique empty-tuple bijection.

For E∈𝔅 set S_E={i:a_i≤E}. Boolean algebra gives

    E = ∩_{i∉S_E} K_i.

Thus h carries E precisely to agreement outside S_E. Set S_p=S_{E_p}.
Agreement on S_p corresponds to E_p^*, which lies in H_p by (B), so P_p is
local to S_p. Finally, the typed recursion in (G) matches the coordinate
support recursion by induction. Consequently all R_A are represented, not
only the atomic relations. Induction on consequent formulas preserves nested
conditionals as well. □

The geometric argument above is supplied in full to make its use inspectable;
it is standard Boolean factor-equivalence decomposition. The theorem's
application is to the combined observable atomic data, local colors, and
exact-verifier conditional extension.

### Rigidity despite nonunique coordinates

**Corollary 2.** If finite atomic data admit a representation, they admit
exactly one raw-variation extension A↦R_A on the free normal syntax. Different
admissible coordinate decompositions yield the same relations for every A,
and therefore the same truth values for every right-nested conditional.

**Proof.** E_p is recovered from R_p and R_¬p. The typed recursion in (G) uses
only E_p, P_p, lattice join, and set operations; none depends on a completion.
Apply Theorem 1 to each representation. □

This does not say that an arbitrary single conditional relation identifies its
verifier family. On one Boolean coordinate, the update families {x←0,x←1}
and {identity,x←0,x←1} have identical endpoint relations. The corollary instead
uses all named **atomic positive and negative** relations and the stipulated
compositional semantics.

## 4. Obstructions and an explicit fragment

The equivalence lattice generated by E_p, Δ, U must be distributive, uniform
and pairwise commuting in any representation. These are useful necessary
geometric tests, but truth labels introduce additional constraints.

**Four-world color obstruction.** Take E with blocks {0,1} and {2,3}, and two
atoms p,q both having recovered relation E. Set P_p={0,2}, P_q={0,3}, and
define their positive/negative relations by (A). Every primitive truth set
has one true point in each E-block. Nevertheless the joint truth patterns are

    first block:  (p,q)=(1,1),(0,0);
    second block: (p,q)=(1,0),(0,1).

In any local-support representation, both atoms have the same support: the
map S↦E_S is injective after removing singleton coordinates. Changing the
outside coordinates must preserve their joint truth pattern. Every E-block
would therefore have identical pattern multiplicities, which they do not.
No completion satisfying (B) exists. Checking one predicate at a time misses
this obstruction, even though the geometry is the simplest uniform chain.

**Proposition 3 (complete single-support test).** Suppose m≥1. If all E_p equal the same
equivalence E, atomic data satisfying (A) are representable if and only if
every E-class has the same multiset of joint m-atom truth vectors.

**Proof.** Necessity is the preceding argument. For sufficiency, choose a
reference E-class D. Equal pattern multiplicities allow a truth-vector-
preserving bijection from D to each E-class. Identify W with D×(W/E), assign
every atom support {D}, and interpret its truth from its label on D. These
bijections represent E and every atomic truth set. Then use (G) for the unique
full extension. Empty atom families are handled by a one-coordinate W model. □

If the generated lattice is already Boolean, no completion search is needed:
each existing complement is forced in every Boolean extension. Thus (B)
reduces to checking commutation and E_p^*⊆H_p in that lattice. If the lattice
is not Boolean, a geometry-only poset-block representation is not sufficient
to justify the color condition.

### Why one positive conditional relation is not an antecedent algebra

**Proposition (negation obstruction).** There is no unary operation on positive
endpoint relations that, throughout this semantic class, sends R_A to R_¬A.
This already fails within a single four-world model.

**Proof.** On the Boolean square let p read the first coordinate with that
singleton support, and q the second with its singleton support. Put
A=p∧¬p and B=q∧¬q. Both have empty positive relation. But ¬A has every point
as a verifier with first-coordinate support, so R_¬A=E_{first}; similarly
R_¬B=E_{second}, and these relations differ. A function cannot send the same
empty relation to both outputs. □

Thus a relation-only quotient cannot simply inherit the antecedent negation
operation. A full conditional algebra must retain bilateral or other
intensional information. Theorem 1 avoids the problem by specifying the free
normal syntax and generating both verifier polarities; it does not silently
identify antecedents having equal positive transitions. This is a structural
warning, not a priority claim for a new hyperintensionality phenomenon.

## 5. Finite decision bounds and their limits

**Proposition 4.** If atomic data on N worlds have a representation, one exists
with at most floor(log₂N) nontrivial coordinates. With m atoms one can moreover
use at most min(floor(log₂N),2^m) coordinates.

**Proof.** Discard singleton domains. Each remaining coordinate contributes
at least a factor of two to N. Coordinates with the same membership pattern
in S_{p_1},…,S_{p_m} may be bundled into one product coordinate, preserving
every atomic support relation and locality. There are at most 2^m patterns,
including the pattern omitted by every support (background variation). □

Thus atomic representability is decidable: enumerate bounded Boolean
sublattices of the finite Eq(W), test (B), and reconstruct a witness. This is
an intrinsic finite test, not an efficient algorithm claim.

**Proposition 5 (NP upper bound).** Given N explicitly named worlds, atomic
truth sets and positive/negative relation matrices, and a finite list of
normal formulas with claimed relation matrices, exact simultaneous realization
of these observations on the same worlds belongs to NP.

**Proof.** Guess ≤log₂N coordinates, their domain sizes (product N), a
bijection of W to their product, and m support masks. This has polynomial
size. Check bijectivity, locality and the atomic matrices. Let L be the total
syntax length of the finite formula list. Compute the positive/negative support families at each world.
There are at most 2^k≤N supports; each fusion enumerates at most N² pairs.
The total work is polynomial, e.g. O(LN³ log(2N)+mN² log(2N)) with elementary bit masks and
endpoint comparisons. Compare the computed matrices with the supplied ones.
All finite observations must use the **same** guessed representation. □

This is not an NP claim for satisfiability of the unrestricted logic, nor a
finite-query decision procedure for an arbitrary infinite oracle A↦R_A.
For a complete abstract system, (G) remains a scheme over all formulas unless
the input supplies a finite recursive presentation supporting an equivalence
check. Finite W alone cannot exclude an oracle changing one unqueried formula.

Run `python models/check_intrinsic.py` for the finite verification. It enumerates
all 15 equivalences on four worlds and finds their four bounded Boolean
commuting completions. For all 57,600 pairs of equivalence/color atomic data,
the abstract completion criterion agrees with an independent enumeration of
direct-product bijections (1,152 inputs are representable). It also compares
84,672 formula relation matrices, using 36 explicitly enumerated formulas of
bounded depth, from the intrinsic recursion against the
coordinate evaluator, and checks the joint-color obstruction and singleton
case. These checks support the written proof; they do not replace it.

## 6. What remains

The finite same-world existence problem now has an exact relational criterion;
the expensive remaining ingredient is the colored Boolean completion. Next
questions are an intrinsic elimination of that existential completion, sharp
complexity, and a useful finite axiomatization. Infinite state spaces require
separate hypotheses: the finite quotient-amalgamation proof does not establish
surjectivity onto infinite products. None of these gaps is concealed by the
finite representation theorem.
